# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The core moment is vivid and easy to picture: a spectator's freely chosen word or number appears inside what looks like the phone's own Photos video. Starting from that moment, the product is clear.
- There is real attention to craft: fake system apps styled closely on iOS 26 and Android, Face ID animation, motion tracking and blend modes. The Product lens rewards care in details the audience will look at.
- Delivery is simple for the buyer: no app store, instant in the browser, works offline, on both platforms.
- A free tier lets people try it before paying, which shortens the path to the first good experience.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Seams that break the illusion.** The spectator sees sccs1298.github.io, the real status bar shows the real time, and the free ntfy relay caps at roughly 250 messages a day per IP. In magic, one visible seam ruins the trick and the performer's reputation. The founder's own rule, "must never look suspicious", is not met today.
2. **No focus.** Gallery, cards, mosaic, shattering gallery, six fake system apps, spectator-phone search, smartwatch relay and a Wikipedia trick: the brief cannot say the product in one sentence. The audience ("anyone" or "magicians") and the market (Israel or worldwide) are also undecided. "Best magic app in the world in every parameter" is the opposite of saying no.
3. **The buying experience is unfinished.** The payment terminal is not live, license codes are handed off separately, and the first five minutes after purchase (learning the secret inputs, rehearsing, pairing a watch) are not described. A trick the buyer cannot perform confidently on day one is a refund or a bad review.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one trick, in one sentence, that you would keep if you had to cut everything else?
- Who is the buyer: a non-magician wanting to wow family, or a magician? The setup and teaching differ completely.
- Walk me through the first five minutes after paying 99 ₪: what does the buyer see, learn and perform?
- When will a short own domain replace the github.io address, and what does the spectator see on their screen at every step?
- What happens when the ntfy relay hits its limit mid-performance?
- Is the Wikipedia trick too close to the existing commercial product it resembles? (Open question; not assessed here.)
- What tutorials or practice mode teach the secret input methods?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one hero trick (the Gallery is the natural candidate) and one audience; move or hide the rest until the hero is flawless.
2. Remove the visible seams: own short domain before selling, and a relay that will not fail during a performance (or a clear fallback).
3. Finish and own the whole purchase path: working payment, automatic license delivery, a guided first-run that gets the buyer to a successful performance.
4. Test the hero trick with real first-time buyers and watch where they get stuck before spending on marketing.

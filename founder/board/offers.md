# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The dream outcome is vivid and easy to understand.** "Your phone reads their mind, and it looks like your own Photos app" is a moment a buyer can picture right away.
- **Time to the result is short.** It works instantly in the browser, offline, on iPhone and Android, and some tricks are free. A buyer can try it before paying.
- **There is already a deep stack.** The gallery, the fake system apps, the spectator's own phone with a smartwatch link, and the Wikipedia trick are many solutions under one price, and each extra trick costs almost nothing to deliver.
- **Delivery costs are close to zero.** Free hosting, instant digital delivery and a one-time payment mean most of the 99 ₪ is margin, if the free relay holds up.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The market is the weakest link.** Phone magic is a want, not a massive pain. Hebrew-speaking hobbyists are a small pool, and the brief gives no sign that they are easy to reach or growing. With one payment per buyer, 100,000 ₪ a month means about 1,010 new buyers every month. The brief has no sales, no customer acquisition cost and no marketing budget to show that this is possible.
2. **The buyer has little reason to believe it will work.** The promise is "never looks suspicious", yet the spectator sees a sccs1298.github.io address, and the real status bar shows the real time. There is no proof (videos, testimonials, sales) and no guarantee. A buyer who is unsure the trick will fool their friends will not pay.
3. **The effort is high for the "anyone" buyer.** Secret input methods, pairing two phones and setting up ntfy on a watch take practice. A casual buyer who fails in front of friends gets the opposite of the dream outcome. Without a clear target buyer, the offer cannot be built to lower this effort.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly has the strongest desire and money: casual Hebrew speakers, or hobby and professional magicians? Where do you reach them, and what does that cost?
- What proof will you show before people buy: performance videos, reactions, early buyers?
- How long does a first-time buyer take to perform one trick well? What do they have to learn?
- What is the guarantee, and can the margin afford it?
- Could you charge double and still be the obvious choice? What would have to be added to the stack for that?
- What happens to the experience when the ntfy free tier (about 250 messages a day per IP) runs out during a show?
- Competitors' prices are not known: is 99 ₪ cheap, fair or high next to the alternatives?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick one buyer** (start with Hebrew-speaking hobby magicians, who are easier to find and already spend on tricks) and write the offer for them.
2. **Lower the risk and the effort:** buy a short own domain, add a "perform it in 5 minutes" first trick with a guided tutorial, and offer a clear guarantee that matches the margin.
3. **Prove demand before you scale:** get the first paying buyers through the payment terminal, with real performance videos, and measure what each buyer costs to acquire before you decide on worldwide.

# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market it could own first.** Hebrew-language phone magic is a narrow niche. If no Hebrew-native product serves it well (not yet checked), being first and best there is the "big share of a small market" start this lens wants.
- **Depth of craft.** Much is already live: fake system apps styled per OS, spectator-phone-to-smartwatch pairing, offline use. A bundle of know-how, not one trick.
- **Software economics.** Near-zero cost per sale, so scale helps margins if distribution works.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No distribution.** No sales, no marketing budget, no known customer acquisition cost. The goal of about 1,010 sales a month assumes a channel that does not exist yet.
2. **Not a 10x breakthrough, and easy to copy.** The brief says the Wikipedia trick works like an existing product (WikiTest). The rest is a web app built with an AI coding assistant on free infrastructure, so a competitor could rebuild the visible tricks in months. It cannot control the real phone, which caps it against native apps. Nothing yet points to proprietary technology, network effects or a brand.
3. **Dependence on platforms the founder does not control.** 1:1 iOS 26 and Android styling breaks with every OS redesign. Free GitHub Pages, the ntfy.sh limit (about 250 messages a day per IP) and the visible github.io address undercut the "never suspicious" promise.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who else sells phone magic to Hebrew speakers, and at what price? (Competitors are not mapped yet.)
- What is the secret: what do you know about this audience or method that others do not?
- How do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each cost?
- Which small market first: Israeli hobby and pro magicians, or the general public? Each needs a different channel.
- What stops a copycat in month six, and what still holds after several OS redesigns?
- Why now?
- Does 99 ₪ one time fund ongoing OS-styling upkeep, or is recurring revenue needed?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick the beachhead.** One small market (for example, Hebrew-speaking magicians in Israel), with its competitors mapped before more tricks are built.
2. **Prove distribution.** A repeatable channel with real sales and a measured cost per customer before any worldwide expansion.
3. **Name the moat.** At least one durable edge: a brand inside the Israeli magic community, a method genuinely ahead rather than a remake, or a hard-to-copy community.
4. **Own the infrastructure.** An own short domain, and no hard ntfy limit on the core promise.
